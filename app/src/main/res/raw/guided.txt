# Guided sessions, written for the app and read aloud by the phone's own voice.
# S|key|title|subtitle|short minutes|long minutes|suggested sound key
# Then one line per step: seconds of quiet after it|text   (a leading "L:" means the step is only in the long version)
# The long version also doubles every pause.

S|breathing|Breathing space|A short pause in three steps|3|7|stream
4|Find a way of sitting or lying that feels steady. You can close your eyes, or let them rest on one spot.
6|This is a short pause in three steps. There's nothing to get right.
3|First, notice what is here right now.
10|What thoughts are passing through? You don't need to follow them. Just notice that they are there.
10|What feelings are here? Perhaps something pleasant, something difficult, or nothing much at all.
10|And what can you feel in your body? Any tightness, warmth, heaviness, or ease.
L:12|Whatever you find, let it be as it is for now. You are only taking a look.
4|Now, gently, gather your attention onto your breathing.
12|Feel the breath coming in, and the breath going out, wherever you notice it most.
12|When your mind wanders, and it will, simply notice where it went, and come back to the next breath.
L:20|There's no need to change the breath. Let it find its own rhythm.
L:20|In, and out. Each breath a small place to rest.
4|Now let your attention widen, from the breath to the whole body.
12|Feel the body sitting or lying here, breathing. The weight of it. The space around it.
L:15|If there is any discomfort, you might breathe towards it, and on the out-breath let it soften, just a little.
8|As this pause comes to an end, take this wider awareness with you into whatever comes next.
3|Thank you for taking this time.

S|bodyscan|Body scan|Resting attention, from feet to head|7|15|waves
5|Settle into a comfortable position, lying down if you can. Let your arms rest by your sides.
8|Close your eyes if that feels right. Take a few slower breaths, and let each out-breath be a little longer than the one before.
6|In this practice we will move our attention slowly through the body. You don't have to relax. Just notice what is there.
3|Begin with your feet.
14|Notice the toes, the soles, the heels. Perhaps warmth or coolness, the touch of a blanket or socks, or perhaps no sensation at all. That's fine too.
L:20|Imagine the breath travelling all the way down to the feet, and on the out-breath, let them be heavy.
3|Now move up to the ankles and the lower legs.
12|The shins, the calves. Notice any tingling, or tiredness, or ease.
3|Now the knees, and the thighs.
12|Feel the weight of the legs, resting, supported.
L:20|If your mind has wandered, that's what minds do. Gently bring it back to where you were.
3|Now the hips and the pelvis.
12|Notice where the body meets the surface beneath you. Let it carry your weight.
3|Now the lower back, and the belly.
14|Feel the belly rising as you breathe in, and falling as you breathe out.
L:20|There's nothing to fix. Just this rising, and this falling.
3|Now the chest, and the upper back.
14|Notice the heart beating, if you can feel it. The ribs widening and settling with each breath.
3|Now the hands.
12|The fingers, the palms, the backs of the hands. Perhaps a pulse in the fingertips.
3|Now the arms. The wrists, the forearms, the elbows, the upper arms.
12|Let them be heavy.
3|Now the shoulders.
14|Shoulders often carry a lot. On the next out-breath, let them soften and drop, just a little.
L:20|And again, breathing out, letting go of whatever they don't need to hold.
3|Now the neck and the throat.
12|Notice the breath moving through the throat, cool coming in, warmer going out.
3|Now the face.
14|The jaw, letting the teeth part slightly. The cheeks. The eyes, resting in their sockets. The forehead, smooth and wide.
3|And now the top of the head.
15|Now let your attention spread through the whole body at once, from the top of the head to the soles of the feet, breathing.
L:30|Resting here, as a whole body, breathing, alive.
8|When you are ready, let a little movement come back into your fingers and toes.
4|Take your time, and open your eyes when you choose.

S|tension|Let go of tension|Tense, then release, muscle by muscle|7|13|rain
5|Find a comfortable position, sitting or lying. In this practice you will gently tense a group of muscles for a few seconds, then let go, and notice the difference.
5|Tense only to a mild, comfortable level, never to the point of pain. If any part of your body is injured or sore, simply skip it and just breathe.
6|Take a slow breath in, and a long breath out.
3|Begin with your hands. Make both hands into fists, and squeeze gently.
5|Hold, and notice the tightness.
12|And let go. Let the fingers fall open. Notice how the hands feel now, warm, heavy, loose.
L:15|Once more, squeeze the fists. Hold. And release.
3|Now bend your arms and tense the upper arms, as if showing your muscles.
5|Hold it there.
12|And release. Let the arms fall back and rest.
3|Now raise your shoulders up towards your ears.
5|Hold them there.
14|And let them drop. Feel the shoulders settle further than before.
L:15|Again, shoulders up. Hold. And let them fall.
3|Now the face. Scrunch up your eyes and forehead.
5|Hold.
12|And release. Let the forehead be smooth and the eyes soft.
3|Press your lips together and clench your jaw lightly.
5|Hold.
14|And let go. Let the jaw hang loose, the tongue resting.
3|Now take a deep breath in and hold it, feeling the chest tighten.
5|Hold.
14|And breathe out slowly. Let the chest soften.
3|Now gently pull in your stomach muscles.
5|Hold.
14|And release. Let the belly be soft, breathing freely.
3|Now the legs. Press your knees together and tighten your thighs.
5|Hold.
12|And release.
3|Now point your toes away from you, tightening the calves.
5|Hold.
12|And let go.
3|Now pull your toes up towards you.
5|Hold.
14|And release. Feel the whole of each leg, heavy and loose.
6|Now let your attention move slowly over the whole body. If you find any tension left, breathe towards it, and on the out-breath, let it go.
L:30|Rest here. There's nothing you need to do now.
15|Notice the feeling of a body that has let go. Remember it. You can come back to it whenever you need.
5|When you are ready, take a deeper breath, stretch a little, and open your eyes.

S|kindness|Loving-kindness|Kind wishes, for yourself and others|5|10|bowl
5|Sit comfortably, and let your breathing settle. This practice is about wishing well, quietly, in your own words.
6|It is very old: the Buddha taught it as one of the "divine abodes" of the heart. It doesn't matter whether you feel warmth at first. The intention is enough.
4|Begin with yourself. Silently repeat these phrases, slowly, letting each one land.
10|May I be safe.
10|May I be peaceful.
10|May I be kind to myself.
12|May I accept myself as I am.
L:20|If this feels hard, picture yourself as a small child, and offer the wishes to that child.
5|Now bring to mind someone you love easily, a friend, a family member, even a pet. Picture them.
10|May you be safe.
10|May you be peaceful.
10|May you be happy.
12|May you be free from suffering.
5|Now think of someone you hardly know: a neighbour, a shop assistant, someone you passed today. They want to be happy too.
10|May you be safe.
10|May you be peaceful.
12|May you be happy.
L:6|If you are ready, think of someone you find difficult. Not the hardest person, just someone who troubles you a little.
L:12|May you be safe. May you be peaceful.
L:15|May you be free from the suffering that makes you act as you do.
5|Now let the wishes spread out, wider and wider, to everyone in this town, this country, the whole world. To every living being.
12|May all beings be safe.
12|May all beings be peaceful.
15|May all beings be happy and free.
6|Rest for a moment in whatever you feel now.
3|And when you are ready, return.

S|sleep|Into sleep|A slow wind-down to drift off with|5|11|rain
5|Lie down in bed, and let your head sink into the pillow.
8|There is nothing more to do today. Whatever was left undone can wait until tomorrow. It will still be there, and so will you.
6|Take a slow breath in, and let it out with a sigh.
10|And again. Breathing in, and a long, slow breath out.
6|Let your eyes be heavy. Let your jaw be loose.
12|Feel the weight of your body on the bed. The bed holds all of you. You don't have to hold yourself up.
L:20|Each time you breathe out, let yourself sink a little deeper into it.
6|Now let your breathing slow down by itself. Don't force it. Just notice it getting a little slower, a little softer.
15|In, and out.
L:25|In, and out.
6|If thoughts come, that's alright. Picture each one as a leaf on a slow river. Watch it drift by, and let it go.
20|There's nowhere to go. Nothing to solve.
6|Now picture a place where you feel completely safe. A real place, or one you imagine.
15|Notice the light there. The colours. The sounds. The air on your skin.
L:30|Let yourself stay there, as long as you like.
6|Your hands are heavy and warm.
10|Your legs are heavy and warm.
12|Your whole body is heavy, warm and still.
L:30|Sinking, softly, into rest.
8|You can stop listening now, whenever you like, and let sleep come in its own time.
20|Goodnight.

S|morning|A gentle start|Arriving in the day, kindly|4|8|birds
4|Before the day begins, take a moment here. Sit up, or stay lying down if you are still in bed.
8|Take a slow breath in, and let it go. There's no hurry yet.
10|Notice that you are here. A new day, that hasn't happened before.
L:15|Feel the air on your face, the weight of your body, the light coming in, however much there is.
4|Stretch a little, if you like. Your arms, your neck, your back.
10|Whatever kind of night it was, let it be over now.
6|Now think of one thing you are looking forward to today. It can be very small. A cup of tea. A walk. A voice you'll hear.
12|Let yourself feel a little of that, already.
L:6|And think of one thing that might be hard today.
L:15|You don't have to solve it now. Just know that you will meet it as it comes, one part at a time.
6|Now choose one way you would like to be today. Patient, perhaps. Or kind. Or simply present.
12|Say it to yourself, quietly, as a gentle intention, not a rule.
L:20|If the day pulls you away from it, as days do, you can come back to it at any moment, with one breath.
6|Take one more slow breath, a little deeper than the others.
3|And go gently into your day.

S|compassion|A kind hand|For a hard day: kindness towards yourself|5|10|bowl
5|Find a comfortable way to sit or lie down. This is a short pause for when things are hard.
8|Take a slow breath, and as you breathe out, let your shoulders soften.
5|Bring to mind what is difficult right now. Not the hardest thing in your life, just what is weighing on you today.
12|Notice how it feels in your body. Perhaps a tightness in the chest, a heaviness, a knot in the stomach.
5|Now say to yourself, gently: this is a moment of suffering. This is hard.
10|You don't have to pretend it isn't.
5|And remember: everyone struggles. Others feel this too. You are not alone in it, even if it feels that way.
10|Somewhere, right now, many people feel just as you do.
5|Now, if it feels right, place a hand on your heart, or on your cheek, or wherever is comforting.
12|Feel the warmth of your hand. Let it be a kind hand, the way you would comfort a friend.
L:20|Breathe with it. In, and out. Let the warmth spread a little.
5|Now offer yourself some words of kindness. Perhaps: may I be kind to myself. May I give myself what I need.
12|Or find your own words, whatever you would say to someone you love who was going through this.
L:20|If being kind to yourself feels strange, or even hard, that's alright. Just notice that, and let the wish be there anyway.
8|Stay here for a few more breaths, with your hand, and your kindness.
4|When you are ready, take a deeper breath, and go on with your day as gently as you can.

S|leaves|Leaves on a stream|Watching thoughts float by|5|10|stream
5|Sit comfortably, and let your eyes close, or rest them on one spot.
8|Take a few slow breaths, and let your body settle.
6|Now imagine you are sitting beside a gentle stream. The water is slow and clear.
12|Leaves are floating past on the surface, one after another, carried by the current.
L:15|You might hear the water. Feel the ground beneath you. See the light on the stream.
6|Now, each time a thought comes into your mind, place it on a leaf, and watch it float away.
15|It might be a word, a picture, a worry, a plan. Whatever it is, put it on a leaf and let the stream carry it.
6|You don't need to push the leaves along, or make them go faster. Just let them drift at their own pace.
15|If no thoughts come, simply watch the stream, and wait.
5|If you notice that you've been swept away by a thought, that's completely normal.
12|Gently come back to the bank, and put that thought on a leaf too.
L:6|Even thoughts like this is boring, or I'm doing it wrong, can go on a leaf.
L:25|Sitting by the stream. Leaf after leaf. Thought after thought, floating by.
5|You are not your thoughts. You are the one watching them come and go.
15|Rest here a little longer, beside the water.
6|Now let the picture of the stream fade, and come back to the room. Notice the sounds around you.
4|And open your eyes when you are ready.

S|wave|Riding the wave|When a strong feeling rises|4|8|waves
4|If a strong feeling is here right now, panic, anger, fear or sadness, you don't have to fight it.
6|Feelings rise and fall like waves. Even the biggest wave passes.
6|Plant your feet on the floor. Feel the ground holding you up.
10|Breathe out slowly, longer than you breathe in. And again.
5|Now notice where the feeling is in your body. Your chest, your throat, your stomach, your hands.
12|Describe it to yourself, as if you were curious about it. Is it hot or cold? Tight or buzzing? Still or moving?
5|You don't have to like it. Just let it be there, and watch it.
12|Notice that it is changing, moment by moment. A little stronger, then a little less.
L:20|Imagine you are on a surfboard, riding the feeling. It lifts you up, and then it lets you down.
5|Keep breathing slowly. Breathe around the feeling, giving it space.
12|You can feel this, and still be safe. This is a wave, and you are riding it.
L:20|If the feeling grows, that's the wave cresting. Stay with your breath. It will come down.
6|Notice where the wave is now. Has it changed, even a little?
10|Whatever is left, let it be. It will keep passing.
4|Take one more slow breath, and when you are ready, carry on, gently.

S|backtosleep|Back to sleep|For waking in the night|4|9|tent
5|You've woken in the night. That's alright. It happens to everyone, and sleep will come back.
8|You don't need to check the time. Whatever time it is, it's a time for resting.
6|Let your body lie heavy on the bed. Let the pillow hold your head.
12|Breathe out slowly, and let your jaw loosen. Let your tongue rest. Let your eyes be soft behind their lids.
6|If thoughts are busy, that's alright. Night thoughts always seem bigger than they are. In the morning they will be their usual size.
12|You can let them wait until then.
6|Now let's count the breath, very slowly. Breathing in, and breathing out, is one.
10|Breathing in, and out, two.
10|In, and out, three.
L:12|Four.
L:15|Five.
6|If you lose count, it doesn't matter. Just begin again at one.
20|Even resting like this, without sleeping, is good for you. Your body is resting, whether you sleep or not.
L:25|Let each breath out be a little slower, a little softer.
8|There's nothing to do. Nowhere to be until the morning.
20|You can stop listening now, and drift.

S|walking|Walking slowly|A short walk, step by step|5|10|wind
5|This is a walk to take slowly, indoors or outside. Find a quiet stretch where you can walk up and down, ten or twenty steps.
6|Stand still first, and feel your feet on the ground. Feel your weight, and the way your body balances.
5|Now begin to walk, more slowly than usual.
12|Notice the lifting of one foot, the moving, and the placing of it down. Then the other.
6|You might say to yourself, quietly: lifting, moving, placing.
15|Let your arms hang loosely, or hold your hands together, in front or behind.
L:20|Feel the heel touch the ground, then the sole, then the toes. The weight shifting from one leg to the other.
6|When you reach the end of your path, stop for a moment. Then turn slowly, and walk back.
15|If your mind wanders, that's fine. Notice where it went, and come back to the feeling of your feet.
6|Now widen your attention a little. Notice the sounds around you, the air, the light.
15|Keep walking, slowly, feeling each step.
L:6|There's nowhere to get to. Each step is already the place you're going.
L:25|Lifting, moving, placing.
6|Now let your steps slow even more, and come to a stop.
8|Stand still, and feel your whole body standing here, breathing.
3|Thank you for this walk.

S|mountain|The mountain|Steady as a mountain, whatever the weather|5|10|wind
5|Sit tall and steady, with your feet on the floor and your hands resting in your lap. Let your eyes close.
8|Take a few slow breaths, and feel the weight of your body, settled and supported.
6|Now picture a mountain. One you know, or one you imagine. See its broad base, rooted deep in the earth, and its peak rising into the sky.
12|Notice how still it is. It has stood there for thousands of years.
L:15|See its slopes, its rocks and trees, perhaps snow at the top, streams running down its sides.
6|Now let the mountain and your body become one. Your seat is its broad base. Your spine rises like its slopes. Your head is the peak.
12|Feel how steady you are, how rooted. Breathing quietly, like a mountain.
6|Over the mountain, the weather changes. Sun and cloud, rain and wind, snow, then sunshine again.
12|Storms gather and pass. The seasons turn. And through it all, the mountain stays itself.
L:20|Clouds may hide the peak for days. People may praise it or ignore it. The mountain does not change because of it.
6|Our moods and thoughts are like the weather. Worry blows in, sadness rains, joy shines out. All of it moves across us and passes.
15|You can feel the weather fully, and still be the mountain underneath.
L:25|Sit here a little longer, still and steady, letting whatever weather is here today come and go.
6|When you are ready, take a deeper breath, and bring a little of the mountain's steadiness with you.
3|Open your eyes when you choose.

S|gratitude|Thank you|A quiet few minutes of gratitude|4|8|bowl
4|Find a comfortable way to sit, and let your breathing slow down by itself.
8|In this short practice, we will simply notice some of what we are grateful for. It doesn't need to be big.
5|Begin with your body. Something that is working for you right now. Your breath, coming and going without effort. Your heart, beating.
12|Silently say thank you to it.
5|Now think of something small from today, or yesterday. A warm drink. A kind word. Light through a window. A song you heard.
12|Let yourself remember it in detail, and feel a little of the pleasure again.
L:6|Think of a place you are glad to know. A room, a park, a view, a corner of the world.
L:15|Picture it now, and let gratitude rest there for a moment.
5|Now bring to mind a person you are grateful for. Someone in your life now, or someone from the past.
12|Think of one thing they did, or one way they are, that you are thankful for.
L:15|If you like, imagine telling them so. Notice how it feels to say it.
6|Finally, think of something difficult that has also given you something: a lesson, a strength, a friend you found through it.
12|You don't have to be grateful for the hardship itself. Just for what grew alongside it.
6|Rest for a moment in this feeling of having been given things. Of not being alone.
3|And when you are ready, open your eyes, and carry a little of it with you.

S|safeplace|A safe place|Rest in a calm place of your own|5|10|stream
5|Settle into a comfortable position, and close your eyes if that feels right.
8|Take a slow breath in, and a long breath out. And again.
5|In this practice you will picture a place where you feel completely calm and safe. It can be somewhere real, or a place you invent.
10|Let a place come to mind. A beach, a garden, a forest, a room you love. Don't worry if it changes; just choose one for now.
6|Begin to look around. What do you see? Notice the colours, the light, the shapes nearby and far away.
12|Take your time.
6|Now listen. What can you hear in this place? Perhaps water, or wind, or birds, or simply quiet.
12|Let the sounds come to you.
6|Notice what you can feel. The ground beneath you. The air on your skin, warm or cool.
12|And any scents in the air. Grass, sea, rain, wood smoke, flowers.
L:20|Find a spot in this place where you would like to rest, and settle there.
6|Here, nothing is asked of you. You are safe, and you can simply be.
15|Let your body soften in this feeling of safety.
L:25|If you like, give your place a single word, a name that will bring it back to you whenever you need it.
6|This place is always here. You can come back to it at any time, just by closing your eyes and breathing.
8|Now slowly let the picture fade, and notice the room around you again.
3|Open your eyes when you are ready.
