B|gitanjali|Gitanjali|Rabindranath Tagore|tr. by the author, 1912|Song offerings|C07A2E|103 songs
I|Rabindranath Tagore (1861 to 1941) was a Bengali poet, songwriter, painter, novelist and teacher, who founded a school under the trees at Santiniketan. Gitanjali means 'song offerings'. In 1912 he translated a selection of his Bengali songs into English prose himself, on the voyage to England and after; W. B. Yeats wrote the introduction, and the next year Tagore became the first non-European to win the Nobel Prize in Literature.
I|These are those 103 prose poems, in Tagore's own English. Many began as songs that are still sung in Bengal; two of his songs became the national anthems of India and Bangladesh.
A|What it is|A book of devotion, but not of doctrine. The poems speak to 'thee', a God who is lover, friend, master, guest and the life of the world all at once, and they move between longing, waiting, joy and the thought of death. The speaker is a singer who has come to offer his songs, and often feels he has not yet sung the one he came to sing.
A|Its great themes|God is found in ordinary life, not away from it: 'He is there where the tiller is tilling the hard ground and where the pathmaker is breaking stones.' Freedom comes not from renouncing the world but through it: 'Deliverance is not for me in renunciation.' The same stream of life that runs through the world's veins 'runs through my veins night and day'.
A|Its great themes|Death, in the last poems, is not an enemy but a guest at the door, to whom the poet will offer 'the full vessel of my life'.
A|How to read it|A few poems at a time, slowly. Each is short enough to read twice, and Tagore's English, simple and rhythmic, carries best read aloud. Some readers open at random, as with the Tao Te Ching. Poem 35, 'Where the mind is without fear', is the most famous, a prayer for his country that reads now as a prayer for anyone.
A|Why it matters|Yeats wrote that he carried the manuscript with him for days, reading it in trains and buses and restaurants, and often had to close it 'lest some stranger would see how much it moved me'. It opened the West to modern Indian literature, and its mixture of joy and humility has made it a book people turn to at weddings, in illness and in grief.

X|35 · Where the mind is without fear and the…|Faith and doubt
C|1|1 · Thou hast made me endless|
T|Thou hast made me endless, such is thy pleasure. This frail vessel thou emptiest again and again, and fillest it ever with fresh life.
T|This little flute of a reed thou hast carried over hills and dales, and hast breathed through it melodies eternally new.
T|At the immortal touch of thy hands my little heart loses its limits in joy and gives birth to utterance ineffable.
T|Thy infinite gifts come to me only on these very small hands of mine. Ages pass, and still thou pourest, and still there is room to fill.
C|2|2 · When thou commandest me to sing it seems…|
T|When thou commandest me to sing it seems that my heart would break with pride; and I look to thy face, and tears come to my eyes.
T|All that is harsh and dissonant in my life melts into one sweet harmony—and my adoration spreads wings like a glad bird on its flight across the sea.
T|I know thou takest pleasure in my singing. I know that only as a singer I come before thy presence.
T|I touch by the edge of the far-spreading wing of my song thy feet which I could never aspire to reach.
T|Drunk with the joy of singing I forget myself and call thee friend who art my lord.
C|3|3 · I know not how thou singest|
T|I know not how thou singest, my master! I ever listen in silent amazement.
T|The light of thy music illumines the world. The life breath of thy music runs from sky to sky. The holy stream of thy music breaks through all stony obstacles and rushes on.
T|My heart longs to join in thy song, but vainly struggles for a voice. I would speak, but speech breaks not into song, and I cry out baffled. Ah, thou hast made my heart captive in the endless meshes of thy music, my master!
C|4|4 · Life of my life|
T|Life of my life, I shall ever try to keep my body pure, knowing that thy living touch is upon all my limbs.
T|I shall ever try to keep all untruths out from my thoughts, knowing that thou art that truth which has kindled the light of reason in my mind.
T|I shall ever try to drive all evils away from my heart and keep my love in flower, knowing that thou hast thy seat in the inmost shrine of my heart.
T|And it shall be my endeavour to reveal thee in my actions, knowing it is thy power gives me strength to act.
C|5|5 · I ask for a moment’s indulgence to sit…|
T|I ask for a moment’s indulgence to sit by thy side. The works that I have in hand I will finish afterwards.
T|Away from the sight of thy face my heart knows no rest nor respite, and my work becomes an endless toil in a shoreless sea of toil.
T|Today the summer has come at my window with its sighs and murmurs; and the bees are plying their minstrelsy at the court of the flowering grove.
T|Now it is time to sit quite, face to face with thee, and to sing dedication of life in this silent and overflowing leisure.
C|6|6 · Pluck this little flower and take it|
T|Pluck this little flower and take it, delay not! I fear lest it droop and drop into the dust.
T|I may not find a place in thy garland, but honour it with a touch of pain from thy hand and pluck it. I fear lest the day end before I am aware, and the time of offering go by.
T|Though its colour be not deep and its smell be faint, use this flower in thy service and pluck it while there is time.
C|7|7 · My song has put off her adornments|
T|My song has put off her adornments. She has no pride of dress and decoration. Ornaments would mar our union; they would come between thee and me; their jingling would drown thy whispers.
T|My poet’s vanity dies in shame before thy sight. O master poet, I have sat down at thy feet. Only let me make my life simple and straight, like a flute of reed for thee to fill with music.
C|8|8 · The child who is decked with prince’s robes…|
T|The child who is decked with prince’s robes and who has jewelled chains round his neck loses all pleasure in his play; his dress hampers him at every step.
T|In fear that it may be frayed, or stained with dust he keeps himself from the world, and is afraid even to move.
T|Mother, it is no gain, thy bondage of finery, if it keep one shut off from the healthful dust of the earth, if it rob one of the right of entrance to the great fair of common human life.
C|9|9 · O Fool|
T|O Fool, try to carry thyself upon thy own shoulders! O beggar, to come beg at thy own door!
T|Leave all thy burdens on his hands who can bear all, and never look behind in regret.
T|Thy desire at once puts out the light from the lamp it touches with its breath. It is unholy—take not thy gifts through its unclean hands. Accept only what is offered by sacred love.
C|10|10 · Here is thy footstool and there rest thy…|
T|Here is thy footstool and there rest thy feet where live the poorest, and lowliest, and lost.
T|When I try to bow to thee, my obeisance cannot reach down to the depth where thy feet rest among the poorest, and lowliest, and lost.
T|Pride can never approach to where thou walkest in the clothes of the humble among the poorest, and lowliest, and lost.
T|My heart can never find its way to where thou keepest company with the companionless among the poorest, the lowliest, and the lost.
C|11|11 · Leave this chanting and singing and telling of…|
T|Leave this chanting and singing and telling of beads! Whom dost thou worship in this lonely dark corner of a temple with doors all shut? Open thine eyes and see thy God is not before thee!
T|He is there where the tiller is tilling the hard ground and where the pathmaker is breaking stones. He is with them in sun and in shower, and his garment is covered with dust. Put of thy holy mantle and even like him come down on the dusty soil!
T|Deliverance? Where is this deliverance to be found? Our master himself has joyfully taken upon him the bonds of creation; he is bound with us all for ever.
T|Come out of thy meditations and leave aside thy flowers and incense! What harm is there if thy clothes become tattered and stained? Meet him and stand by him in toil and in sweat of thy brow.
C|12|12 · The time that my journey takes is long…|
T|The time that my journey takes is long and the way of it long.
T|I came out on the chariot of the first gleam of light, and pursued my voyage through the wildernesses of worlds leaving my track on many a star and planet.
T|It is the most distant course that comes nearest to thyself, and that training is the most intricate which leads to the utter simplicity of a tune.
T|The traveller has to knock at every alien door to come to his own, and one has to wander through all the outer worlds to reach the innermost shrine at the end.
T|My eyes strayed far and wide before I shut them and said “Here art thou!”
T|The question and the cry “Oh, where?” melt into tears of a thousand streams and deluge the world with the flood of the assurance “I am!”
C|13|13 · The song that I came to sing remains…|
T|The song that I came to sing remains unsung to this day.
T|I have spent my days in stringing and in unstringing my instrument.
T|The time has not come true, the words have not been rightly set; only there is the agony of wishing in my heart.
T|The blossom has not opened; only the wind is sighing by.
T|I have not seen his face, nor have I listened to his voice; only I have heard his gentle footsteps from the road before my house.
T|The livelong day has passed in spreading his seat on the floor; but the lamp has not been lit and I cannot ask him into my house.
T|I live in the hope of meeting with him; but this meeting is not yet.
C|14|14 · My desires are many and my cry is…|
T|My desires are many and my cry is pitiful, but ever didst thou save me by hard refusals; and this strong mercy has been wrought into my life through and through.
T|Day by day thou art making me worthy of the simple, great gifts that thou gavest to me unasked—this sky and the light, this body and the life and the mind—saving me from perils of overmuch desire.
T|There are times when I languidly linger and times when I awaken and hurry in search of my goal; but cruelly thou hidest thyself from before me.
T|Day by day thou art making me worthy of thy full acceptance by refusing me ever and anon, saving me from perils of weak, uncertain desire.
C|15|15 · I am here to sing thee songs|
T|I am here to sing thee songs. In this hall of thine I have a corner seat.
T|In thy world I have no work to do; my useless life can only break out in tunes without a purpose.
T|When the hour strikes for thy silent worship at the dark temple of midnight, command me, my master, to stand before thee to sing.
T|When in the morning air the golden harp is tuned, honour me, commanding my presence.
C|16|16 · I have had my invitation to this world’s…|
T|I have had my invitation to this world’s festival, and thus my life has been blessed. My eyes have seen and my ears have heard.
T|It was my part at this feast to play upon my instrument, and I have done all I could.
T|Now, I ask, has the time come at last when I may go in and see thy face and offer thee my silent salutation?
C|17|17 · I am only waiting for love to give…|
T|I am only waiting for love to give myself up at last into his hands. That is why it is so late and why I have been guilty of such omissions.
T|They come with their laws and their codes to bind me fast; but I evade them ever, for I am only waiting for love to give myself up at last into his hands.
T|People blame me and call me heedless; I doubt not they are right in their blame.
T|The market day is over and work is all done for the busy. Those who came to call me in vain have gone back in anger. I am only waiting for love to give myself up at last into his hands.
C|18|18 · Clouds heap upon clouds and it darkens|
T|Clouds heap upon clouds and it darkens. Ah, love, why dost thou let me wait outside at the door all alone?
T|In the busy moments of the noontide work I am with the crowd, but on this dark lonely day it is only for thee that I hope.
T|If thou showest me not thy face, if thou leavest me wholly aside, I know not how I am to pass these long, rainy hours.
T|I keep gazing on the far-away gloom of the sky, and my heart wanders wailing with the restless wind.
C|19|19 · If thou speakest not I will fill my…|
T|If thou speakest not I will fill my heart with thy silence and endure it. I will keep still and wait like the night with starry vigil and its head bent low with patience.
T|The morning will surely come, the darkness will vanish, and thy voice pour down in golden streams breaking through the sky.
T|Then thy words will take wing in songs from every one of my birds’ nests, and thy melodies will break forth in flowers in all my forest groves.
C|20|20 · On the day when the lotus bloomed|
T|On the day when the lotus bloomed, alas, my mind was straying, and I knew it not. My basket was empty and the flower remained unheeded.
T|Only now and again a sadness fell upon me, and I started up from my dream and felt a sweet trace of a strange fragrance in the south wind.
T|That vague sweetness made my heart ache with longing and it seemed to me that is was the eager breath of the summer seeking for its completion.
T|I knew not then that it was so near, that it was mine, and that this perfect sweetness had blossomed in the depth of my own heart.
C|21|21 · I must launch out my boat|
T|I must launch out my boat. The languid hours pass by on the shore—Alas for me!
T|The spring has done its flowering and taken leave. And now with the burden of faded futile flowers I wait and linger.
T|The waves have become clamorous, and upon the bank in the shady lane the yellow leaves flutter and fall.
T|What emptiness do you gaze upon! Do you not feel a thrill passing through the air with the notes of the far-away song floating from the other shore?
C|22|22 · In the deep shadows of the rainy July|
T|In the deep shadows of the rainy July, with secret steps, thou walkest, silent as night, eluding all watchers.
T|Today the morning has closed its eyes, heedless of the insistent calls of the loud east wind, and a thick veil has been drawn over the ever-wakeful blue sky.
T|The woodlands have hushed their songs, and doors are all shut at every house. Thou art the solitary wayfarer in this deserted street. Oh my only friend, my best beloved, the gates are open in my house—do not pass by like a dream.
C|23|23 · Art thou abroad on this stormy night on…|
T|Art thou abroad on this stormy night on thy journey of love, my friend? The sky groans like one in despair.
T|I have no sleep tonight. Ever and again I open my door and look out on the darkness, my friend!
T|I can see nothing before me. I wonder where lies thy path!
T|By what dim shore of the ink-black river, by what far edge of the frowning forest, through what mazy depth of gloom art thou threading thy course to come to me, my friend?
C|24|24 · If the day is done|
T|If the day is done, if birds sing no more, if the wind has flagged tired, then draw the veil of darkness thick upon me, even as thou hast wrapt the earth with the coverlet of sleep and tenderly closed the petals of the drooping lotus at dusk.
T|From the traveller, whose sack of provisions is empty before the voyage is ended, whose garment is torn and dustladen, whose strength is exhausted, remove shame and poverty, and renew his life like a flower under the cover of thy kindly night.
C|25|25 · In the night of weariness let me give…|
T|In the night of weariness let me give myself up to sleep without struggle, resting my trust upon thee.
T|Let me not force my flagging spirit into a poor preparation for thy worship.
T|It is thou who drawest the veil of night upon the tired eyes of the day to renew its sight in a fresher gladness of awakening.
C|26|26 · He came and sat by my side but…|
T|He came and sat by my side but I woke not. What a cursed sleep it was, O miserable me!
T|He came when the night was still; he had his harp in his hands, and my dreams became resonant with its melodies.
T|Alas, why are my nights all thus lost? Ah, why do I ever miss his sight whose breath touches my sleep?
C|27|27 · Light|
T|Light, oh where is the light? Kindle it with the burning fire of desire!
T|There is the lamp but never a flicker of a flame—is such thy fate, my heart? Ah, death were better by far for thee!
T|Misery knocks at thy door, and her message is that thy lord is wakeful, and he calls thee to the love-tryst through the darkness of night.
T|The sky is overcast with clouds and the rain is ceaseless. I know not what this is that stirs in me—I know not its meaning.
T|A moment’s flash of lightning drags down a deeper gloom on my sight, and my heart gropes for the path to where the music of the night calls me.
T|Light, oh where is the light! Kindle it with the burning fire of desire! It thunders and the wind rushes screaming through the void. The night is black as a black stone. Let not the hours pass by in the dark. Kindle the lamp of love with thy life.
C|28|28 · Obstinate are the trammels|
T|Obstinate are the trammels, but my heart aches when I try to break them.
T|Freedom is all I want, but to hope for it I feel ashamed.
T|I am certain that priceless wealth is in thee, and that thou art my best friend, but I have not the heart to sweep away the tinsel that fills my room.
T|The shroud that covers me is a shroud of dust and death; I hate it, yet hug it in love.
T|My debts are large, my failures great, my shame secret and heavy; yet when I come to ask for my good, I quake in fear lest my prayer be granted.
C|29|29 · He whom I enclose with my name is…|
T|He whom I enclose with my name is weeping in this dungeon. I am ever busy building this wall all around; and as this wall goes up into the sky day by day I lose sight of my true being in its dark shadow.
T|I take pride in this great wall, and I plaster it with dust and sand lest a least hole should be left in this name; and for all the care I take I lose sight of my true being.
C|30|30 · I came out alone on my way to…|
T|I came out alone on my way to my tryst. But who is this that follows me in the silent dark?
T|I move aside to avoid his presence but I escape him not.
T|He makes the dust rise from the earth with his swagger; he adds his loud voice to every word that I utter.
T|He is my own little self, my lord, he knows no shame; but I am ashamed to come to thy door in his company.
C|31|31 · “Prisoner|
T|“Prisoner, tell me, who was it that bound you?”
T|“It was my master,” said the prisoner. “I thought I could outdo everybody in the world in wealth and power, and I amassed in my own treasure-house the money due to my king. When sleep overcame me I lay upon the bed that was for my lord, and on waking up I found I was a prisoner in my own treasure-house.”
T|“Prisoner, tell me, who was it that wrought this unbreakable chain?”
T|“It was I,” said the prisoner, “who forged this chain very carefully. I thought my invincible power would hold the world captive leaving me in a freedom undisturbed. Thus night and day I worked at the chain with huge fires and cruel hard strokes. When at last the work was done and the links were complete and unbreakable, I found that it held me in its grip.”
C|32|32 · By all means they try to hold me…|
T|By all means they try to hold me secure who love me in this world. But it is otherwise with thy love which is greater than theirs, and thou keepest me free.
T|Lest I forget them they never venture to leave me alone. But day passes by after day and thou art not seen.
T|If I call not thee in my prayers, if I keep not thee in my heart, thy love for me still waits for my love.
C|33|33 · When it was day they came into my…|
T|When it was day they came into my house and said, “We shall only take the smallest room here.”
T|They said, “We shall help you in the worship of your God and humbly accept only our own share in his grace”; and then they took their seat in a corner and they sat quiet and meek.
T|But in the darkness of night I find they break into my sacred shrine, strong and turbulent, and snatch with unholy greed the offerings from God’s altar.
C|34|34 · Let only that little be left of me…|
T|Let only that little be left of me whereby I may name thee my all.
T|Let only that little be left of my will whereby I may feel thee on every side, and come to thee in everything, and offer to thee my love every moment.
T|Let only that little be left of me whereby I may never hide thee.
T|Let only that little of my fetters be left whereby I am bound with thy will, and thy purpose is carried out in my life—and that is the fetter of thy love.
C|35|35 · Where the mind is without fear and the…|
T|Where the mind is without fear and the head is held high;
T|Where knowledge is free;
T|Where the world has not been broken up into fragments by narrow domestic walls;
T|Where words come out from the depth of truth;
T|Where tireless striving stretches its arms towards perfection;
T|Where the clear stream of reason has not lost its way into the dreary desert sand of dead habit;
T|Where the mind is led forward by thee into ever-widening thought and action—
T|Into that heaven of freedom, my Father, let my country awake.
C|36|36 · This is my prayer to thee|
T|This is my prayer to thee, my lord—strike, strike at the root of penury in my heart.
T|Give me the strength lightly to bear my joys and sorrows.
T|Give me the strength to make my love fruitful in service.
T|Give me the strength never to disown the poor or bend my knees before insolent might.
T|Give me the strength to raise my mind high above daily trifles.
T|And give me the strength to surrender my strength to thy will with love.
C|37|37 · I thought that my voyage had come to…|
T|I thought that my voyage had come to its end at the last limit of my power,—that the path before me was closed, that provisions were exhausted and the time come to take shelter in a silent obscurity.
T|But I find that thy will knows no end in me. And when old words die out on the tongue, new melodies break forth from the heart; and where the old tracks are lost, new country is revealed with its wonders.
C|38|38 · That I want thee|
T|That I want thee, only thee—let my heart repeat without end. All desires that distract me, day and night, are false and empty to the core.
T|As the night keeps hidden in its gloom the petition for light, even thus in the depth of my unconsciousness rings the cry—I want thee, only thee.
T|As the storm still seeks its end in peace when it strikes against peace with all its might, even thus my rebellion strikes against thy love and still its cry is—I want thee, only thee.
C|39|39 · When the heart is hard and parched up|
T|When the heart is hard and parched up, come upon me with a shower of mercy.
T|When grace is lost from life, come with a burst of song.
T|When tumultuous work raises its din on all sides shutting me out from beyond, come to me, my lord of silence, with thy peace and rest.
T|When my beggarly heart sits crouched, shut up in a corner, break open the door, my king, and come with the ceremony of a king.
T|When desire blinds the mind with delusion and dust, O thou holy one, thou wakeful, come with thy light and thy thunder.
C|40|40 · The rain has held back for days and…|
T|The rain has held back for days and days, my God, in my arid heart. The horizon is fiercely naked—not the thinnest cover of a soft cloud, not the vaguest hint of a distant cool shower.
T|Send thy angry storm, dark with death, if it is thy wish, and with lashes of lightning startle the sky from end to end.
T|But call back, my lord, call back this pervading silent heat, still and keen and cruel, burning the heart with dire despair.
T|Let the cloud of grace bend low from above like the tearful look of the mother on the day of the father’s wrath.
C|41|41 · Where dost thou stand behind them all|
T|Where dost thou stand behind them all, my lover, hiding thyself in the shadows? They push thee and pass thee by on the dusty road, taking thee for naught. I wait here weary hours spreading my offerings for thee, while passers-by come and take my flowers, one by one, and my basket is nearly empty.
T|The morning time is past, and the noon. In the shade of evening my eyes are drowsy with sleep. Men going home glance at me and smile and fill me with shame. I sit like a beggar maid, drawing my skirt over my face, and when they ask me, what it is I want, I drop my eyes and answer them not.
T|Oh, how, indeed, could I tell them that for thee I wait, and that thou hast promised to come. How could I utter for shame that I keep for my dowry this poverty. Ah, I hug this pride in the secret of my heart.
T|I sit on the grass and gaze upon the sky and dream of the sudden splendour of thy coming—all the lights ablaze, golden pennons flying over thy car, and they at the roadside standing agape, when they see thee come down from thy seat to raise me from the dust, and set at thy side this ragged beggar girl a-tremble with shame and pride, like a creeper in a summer breeze.
T|But time glides on and still no sound of the wheels of thy chariot. Many a procession passes by with noise and shouts and glamour of glory. Is it only thou who wouldst stand in the shadow silent and behind them all? And only I who would wait and weep and wear out my heart in vain longing?
C|42|42 · Early in the day it was whispered that…|
T|Early in the day it was whispered that we should sail in a boat, only thou and I, and never a soul in the world would know of this our pilgrimage to no country and to no end.
T|In that shoreless ocean, at thy silently listening smile my songs would swell in melodies, free as waves, free from all bondage of words.
T|Is the time not come yet? Are there works still to do? Lo, the evening has come down upon the shore and in the fading light the seabirds come flying to their nests.
T|Who knows when the chains will be off, and the boat, like the last glimmer of sunset, vanish into the night?
C|43|43 · The day was when I did not keep…|
T|The day was when I did not keep myself in readiness for thee; and entering my heart unbidden even as one of the common crowd, unknown to me, my king, thou didst press the signet of eternity upon many a fleeting moment of my life.
T|And today when by chance I light upon them and see thy signature, I find they have lain scattered in the dust mixed with the memory of joys and sorrows of my trivial days forgotten.
T|Thou didst not turn in contempt from my childish play among dust, and the steps that I heard in my playroom are the same that are echoing from star to star.
C|44|44 · This is my delight|
T|This is my delight, thus to wait and watch at the wayside where shadow chases light and the rain comes in the wake of the summer.
T|Messengers, with tidings from unknown skies, greet me and speed along the road. My heart is glad within, and the breath of the passing breeze is sweet.
T|From dawn till dusk I sit here before my door, and I know that of a sudden the happy moment will arrive when I shall see.
T|In the meanwhile I smile and I sing all alone. In the meanwhile the air is filling with the perfume of promise.
C|45|45 · Have you not heard his silent steps|
T|Have you not heard his silent steps? He comes, comes, ever comes.
T|Every moment and every age, every day and every night he comes, comes, ever comes.
T|Many a song have I sung in many a mood of mind, but all their notes have always proclaimed, “He comes, comes, ever comes.”
T|In the fragrant days of sunny April through the forest path he comes, comes, ever comes.
T|In the rainy gloom of July nights on the thundering chariot of clouds he comes, comes, ever comes.
T|In sorrow after sorrow it is his steps that press upon my heart, and it is the golden touch of his feet that makes my joy to shine.
C|46|46 · I know not from what distant time thou…|
T|I know not from what distant time thou art ever coming nearer to meet me. Thy sun and stars can never keep thee hidden from me for aye.
T|In many a morning and eve thy footsteps have been heard and thy messenger has come within my heart and called me in secret.
T|I know not only why today my life is all astir, and a feeling of tremulous joy is passing through my heart.
T|It is as if the time were come to wind up my work, and I feel in the air a faint smell of thy sweet presence.
C|47|47 · The night is nearly spent waiting for him…|
T|The night is nearly spent waiting for him in vain. I fear lest in the morning he suddenly come to my door when I have fallen asleep wearied out. Oh friends, leave the way open to him— forbid him not.
T|If the sounds of his steps does not wake me, do not try to rouse me, I pray. I wish not to be called from my sleep by the clamorous choir of birds, by the riot of wind at the festival of morning light. Let me sleep undisturbed even if my lord comes of a sudden to my door.
T|Ah, my sleep, precious sleep, which only waits for his touch to vanish. Ah, my closed eyes that would open their lids only to the light of his smile when he stands before me like a dream emerging from darkness of sleep.
T|Let him appear before my sight as the first of all lights and all forms. The first thrill of joy to my awakened soul let it come from his glance. And let my return to myself be immediate return to him.

C|50|50 · I had gone a-begging from door to door…|
T|I had gone a-begging from door to door in the village path, when thy golden chariot appeared in the distance like a gorgeous dream and I wondered who was this King of all kings!

T|The night darkened. Our day’s works had been done. We thought that the last guest had arrived for the night and the doors in the village were all shut. Only some said the king was to come. We laughed and said “No, it cannot be!”

C|58|58 · Let all the strains of joy mingle in…|

C|59|59 · Yes|

T|On the seashore of endless worlds children meet. The infinite sky is motionless overhead and the restless water is boisterous. On the seashore of endless worlds the children meet with shouts and dances.

C|62|62 · When I bring to you coloured toys|
T|When I bring to you coloured toys, my child, I understand why there is such a play of colours on clouds, on water, and why flowers are painted in tints—when I give coloured toys to you, my child.

C|72|72 · He it is|

C|73|73 · Deliverance is not for me in renunciation|

C|88|88 · Deity of the ruined temple|
T|Deity of the ruined temple! The broken strings of Vina sing no more your praise. The bells in the evening proclaim not your time of worship. The air is still and silent about you.
T|In your desolate dwelling comes the vagrant spring breeze. It brings the tidings of flowers—the flowers that for your worship are offered no more.
T|Your worshipper of old wanders ever longing for favour still refused. In the eventide, when fires and shadows mingle with the gloom of dust, he wearily comes back to the ruined temple with hunger in his heart.
T|Many a festival day comes to you in silence, deity of the ruined temple. Many a night of worship goes away with lamp unlit.
T|Many new images are built by masters of cunning art and carried to the holy stream of oblivion when their time is come.
T|Only the deity of the ruined temple remains unworshipped in deathless neglect.
C|89|89 · No more noisy|
T|No more noisy, loud words from me—such is my master’s will. Henceforth I deal in whispers. The speech of my heart will be carried on in murmurings of a song.
T|Men hasten to the King’s market. All the buyers and sellers are there. But I have my untimely leave in the middle of the day, in the thick of work.
T|Let then the flowers come out in my garden, though it is not their time; and let the midday bees strike up their lazy hum.
T|Full many an hour have I spent in the strife of the good and the evil, but now it is the pleasure of my playmate of the empty days to draw my heart on to him; and I know not why is this sudden call to what useless inconsequence!
C|90|90 · On the day when death will knock at…|
T|On the day when death will knock at thy door what wilt thou offer to him?
T|Oh, I will set before my guest the full vessel of my life—I will never let him go with empty hands.
T|All the sweet vintage of all my autumn days and summer nights, all the earnings and gleanings of my busy life will I place before him at the close of my days when death will knock at my door.
C|91|91 · O thou the last fulfilment of life|
T|O thou the last fulfilment of life, Death, my death, come and whisper to me!
T|Day after day I have kept watch for thee; for thee have I borne the joys and pangs of life.
T|All that I am, that I have, that I hope and all my love have ever flowed towards thee in depth of secrecy. One final glance from thine eyes and my life will be ever thine own.
T|The flowers have been woven and the garland is ready for the bridegroom. After the wedding the bride shall leave her home and meet her lord alone in the solitude of night.
C|92|92 · I know that the day will come when…|
T|I know that the day will come when my sight of this earth shall be lost, and life will take its leave in silence, drawing the last curtain over my eyes.
T|Yet stars will watch at night, and morning rise as before, and hours heave like sea waves casting up pleasures and pains.
T|When I think of this end of my moments, the barrier of the moments breaks and I see by the light of death thy world with its careless treasures. Rare is its lowliest seat, rare is its meanest of lives.
T|Things that I longed for in vain and things that I got—let them pass. Let me but truly possess the things that I ever spurned and overlooked.
C|93|93 · I have got my leave|
T|I have got my leave. Bid me farewell, my brothers! I bow to you all and take my departure.
T|Here I give back the keys of my door—and I give up all claims to my house. I only ask for last kind words from you.
T|We were neighbours for long, but I received more than I could give. Now the day has dawned and the lamp that lit my dark corner is out. A summons has come and I am ready for my journey.
C|94|94 · At this time of my parting|
T|At this time of my parting, wish me good luck, my friends! The sky is flushed with the dawn and my path lies beautiful.
T|Ask not what I have with me to take there. I start on my journey with empty hands and expectant heart.
T|I shall put on my wedding garland. Mine is not the red-brown dress of the traveller, and though there are dangers on the way I have no fear in mind.
T|The evening star will come out when my voyage is done and the plaintive notes of the twilight melodies be struck up from the King’s gateway.
C|95|95 · I was not aware of the moment when…|
T|I was not aware of the moment when I first crossed the threshold of this life.
T|What was the power that made me open out into this vast mystery like a bud in the forest at midnight!
T|When in the morning I looked upon the light I felt in a moment that I was no stranger in this world, that the inscrutable without name and form had taken me in its arms in the form of my own mother.
T|Even so, in death the same unknown will appear as ever known to me. And because I love this life, I know I shall love death as well.
T|The child cries out when from the right breast the mother takes it away, in the very next moment to find in the left one its consolation.
C|96|96 · When I go from hence let this be…|
T|When I go from hence let this be my parting word, that what I have seen is unsurpassable.
T|I have tasted of the hidden honey of this lotus that expands on the ocean of light, and thus am I blessed—let this be my parting word.
T|In this playhouse of infinite forms I have had my play and here have I caught sight of him that is formless.
T|My whole body and my limbs have thrilled with his touch who is beyond touch; and if the end comes here, let it come—let this be my parting word.
C|97|97 · When my play was with thee I never…|
T|When my play was with thee I never questioned who thou wert. I knew nor shyness nor fear, my life was boisterous.
T|In the early morning thou wouldst call me from my sleep like my own comrade and lead me running from glade to glade.
T|On those days I never cared to know the meaning of songs thou sangest to me. Only my voice took up the tunes, and my heart danced in their cadence.
T|Now, when the playtime is over, what is this sudden sight that is come upon me? The world with eyes bent upon thy feet stands in awe with all its silent stars.
C|98|98 · I will deck thee with trophies|
T|I will deck thee with trophies, garlands of my defeat. It is never in my power to escape unconquered.
T|I surely know my pride will go to the wall, my life will burst its bonds in exceeding pain, and my empty heart will sob out in music like a hollow reed, and the stone will melt in tears.
T|I surely know the hundred petals of a lotus will not remain closed for ever and the secret recess of its honey will be bared.
T|From the blue sky an eye shall gaze upon me and summon me in silence. Nothing will be left for me, nothing whatever, and utter death shall I receive at thy feet.
C|99|99 · When I give up the helm I know…|
T|When I give up the helm I know that the time has come for thee to take it. What there is to do will be instantly done. Vain is this struggle.
T|Then take away your hands and silently put up with your defeat, my heart, and think it your good fortune to sit perfectly still where you are placed.
T|These my lamps are blown out at every little puff of wind, and trying to light them I forget all else again and again.
T|But I shall be wise this time and wait in the dark, spreading my mat on the floor; and whenever it is thy pleasure, my lord, come silently and take thy seat here.
C|100|100 · I dive down into the depth of the…|
T|I dive down into the depth of the ocean of forms, hoping to gain the perfect pearl of the formless.
T|No more sailing from harbour to harbour with this my weather-beaten boat. The days are long passed when my sport was to be tossed on waves.
T|And now I am eager to die into the deathless.
T|Into the audience hall by the fathomless abyss where swells up the music of toneless strings I shall take this harp of my life.
T|I shall tune it to the notes of forever, and when it has sobbed out its last utterance, lay down my silent harp at the feet of the silent.
C|101|101 · Ever in my life have I sought thee…|
T|Ever in my life have I sought thee with my songs. It was they who led me from door to door, and with them have I felt about me, searching and touching my world.
T|It was my songs that taught me all the lessons I ever learnt; they showed me secret paths, they brought before my sight many a star on the horizon of my heart.
T|They guided me all the day long to the mysteries of the country of pleasure and pain, and, at last, to what palace gate have they brought me in the evening at the end of my journey?
C|102|102 · I boasted among men that I had known…|
T|I boasted among men that I had known you. They see your pictures in all works of mine. They come and ask me, “Who is he?” I know not how to answer them. I say, “Indeed, I cannot tell.” They blame me and they go away in scorn. And you sit there smiling.
T|I put my tales of you into lasting songs. The secret gushes out from my heart. They come and ask me, “Tell me all your meanings.” I know not how to answer them. I say, “Ah, who knows what they mean!” They smile and go away in utter scorn. And you sit there smiling.
C|103|103 · In one salutation to thee|
T|In one salutation to thee, my God, let all my senses spread out and touch this world at thy feet.
T|Like a rain-cloud of July hung low with its burden of unshed showers let all my mind bend down at thy door in one salutation to thee.
T|Let all my songs gather together their diverse strains into a single current and flow to a sea of silence in one salutation to thee.
T|Like a flock of homesick cranes flying night and day back to their mountain nests let all my life take its voyage to its eternal home in one salutation to thee.
