therearewondersenoughouttherewithoutourinventingany	From Carl Sagan's The Demon-Haunted World (1995), his defence of science and scepticism against pseudoscience, written shortly before his death in 1996.\n\nSagan argued that reality, honestly examined, is more astonishing than any myth or hoax, and that wonder and doubt belong together.\n\nIt is a good answer to anyone who thinks science takes the magic out of the world.
poetryisanislandthatbreaksawayfromthemain	From "The Antilles: Fragments of Epic Memory", Derek Walcott's Nobel lecture of 1992. He began by describing a performance of the Ramleela, the Hindu epic, in the Trinidad village of Felicity, and spoke of the Caribbean as a broken vase whose pieces, glued together, show its love more than the whole.\n\nFor Walcott, poetry, like an island, is both separate and connected to the whole.\n\nIt is a line about the Caribbean imagination itself: fragments made into new beauty.
